Page 3 · Learning Objectives

After studying this topic, you should be able to:

  • Define governance and identify its importance and key components.
  • Describe how to develop a governance framework and identify the related considerations.
  • Describe the functions of a Center of Excellence (CoE) and explain how to build it.

Page 4 · Introduction

Introduction

A robust governance framework in Salesforce is crucial for aligning projects with business objectives and ensuring effective management. This framework should involve clear rules, processes, and responsibilities, guided by a Center of Excellence (CoE).

The CoE coordinates strategic planning and project execution, ensuring adherence to standards. It harmonizes efforts across various teams, ensuring everyone works towards a common goal and adheres to the established standards. Additionally, effective change management is an integral part of a governance framework since it facilitates smooth transitions and adoption of new processes and technologies.

Fed angle

At a central bank, every one of these concepts is mandatory, not optional. "Clear rules, processes, and responsibilities" plus a CoE that enforces standards maps directly to the Federal Reserve's governance requirements: documented controls, cross-functional oversight, and a change management process that is auditable end-to-end.

Governance

Page 6 · Governance

What is Governance?

Governance is the minimal set of processes needed for a program to deliver business outcomes with the quality required and within the business timeframe.

Unique

Governance is the result of an accumulation of decisions, actions, and planning that are unique to the team, department, and organization.

Focus

It helps the program team focus on the business backlog while adhering to IT policies and best practices.

Partnership

In a comprehensive governance framework, business and IT work together to build and manage mission critical Salesforce solutions.

The components of a governance program fall into four major categories: Center of Excellence (CoE), Change Management, Org Strategy, and Technical Governance. Change Management is the process used to identify, prioritize, assign, execute and communicate changes; the Change Control Board reviews and approves change requests.

Fed angle

The definition — "minimal processes to deliver outcomes within required quality and timeframe" — is the answer when a Fed interviewer asks why governance isn't just bureaucracy. Governance is the minimum needed to deliver reliably; over-governing slows delivery while under-governing creates uncontrolled risk. Calibrate to maturity.

Page 7 · Governance Stages of Maturity

Governance Stages of Maturity

The desired maturity level should be considered when developing a governance framework. There are four stages of maturity.

StageNameCharacteristics
1Non-Existent / BasicLittle or no governance processes; ad hoc decision-making, no standards, changes made directly in production.
2EmergingGovernance awareness is growing; some processes exist but are inconsistently applied across teams.
3PracticingGovernance processes are defined, documented, and consistently followed; CoE in place with clear roles and a program charter.
4LeadingGovernance is optimized and continuously improved; architecture review boards, automated quality gates, full change control, AARs.

Interview angle

When asked "how mature is your current org's governance?" position yourself at Stage 3 heading toward 4: consistent CoE processes, source control as the source of truth, repeatable deployment pipeline — and describe the AARs or retrospectives you use to continuously improve.

Page 8 · Importance of Governance

Importance of Governance

Governance enables innovation, compliance, velocity, delivery, and reduced risk.

Innovation

Governance allows business areas to innovate at their own pace through adoption of agile and new features and functionality.

Compliance

Governance ensures proper data security, visibility, and access in a new solution to meet the compliance requirements.

Velocity

Governance ensures the consistency of prioritization and application roadmap development to drive business velocity.

Delivery

Governance improves the quality of the deliverable through the documentation of best practices for the organization.

Reduced Risk

Governance reduces business risks by delivering what the business requires and when it requires it.

Fed angle

The compliance and reduced-risk dimensions are non-negotiable at the Federal Reserve. Data security, visibility, and access controls are regulatory requirements, not suggestions. Frame every governance conversation through this lens: "which of these five outcomes are we protecting?"

Page 9 · Benefits and Pitfalls

Benefits and Pitfalls of Governance

There are various benefits and pitfalls associated with governance.

Benefits

A good governance framework drives business value, reduces and manages risk, enables improved agility, innovation, and compliance, and ensures timely delivery.

Pitfalls

A poor governance framework causes a lack of alignment between business and IT, low solution adoption, increased costs, reduced value, increased program risk, and slow delivery of capabilities.

The pitfalls list is the exam's "why does governance matter" answer bank. If asked about governance failure modes: misalignment, low adoption, cost overruns, slow delivery, and program risk.
Page 10 · Governance Subdomains

Governance Subdomains

There are several key subdomains that help create governance.

Vision & Strategy

A clear vision and strategy are defined, including business objectives, steps to achieve them, and metrics to measure progress.

Principles

Using the principles of software engineering, a clear development, testing, and QA process is created for all teams.

Business Backlog

Users who will own and prioritize the requirements backlog and what they will consider are defined.

Communication Strategy

A communication strategy is defined to ensure governance alignment across all stakeholders.

Data Architecture

A business-data model is created, and the source of truth, where all data is collected and managed, is determined.

Stakeholder Management

All stakeholders who should help determine or understand the governance framework are defined.

Governance Framework

Page 12 · Developing a Governance Framework (Steps 1–5)

Developing a Governance Framework — Steps 1–5

The following steps are followed to create a governance framework:

StepAction
1The delivery challenges for the program are documented.
2The maturity in the core framework processes is understood.
3A clear vision and strategy are defined, followed by engaging stakeholders and gaining their alignment.
4The business must own and prioritize its requirements.
5The current approach to data architecture and management is reviewed.
Page 13 · Developing a Governance Framework (Steps 6–10)

Developing a Governance Framework — Steps 6–10

The following steps continue the creation of a governance framework:

StepAction
6The basic principles of software engineering are implemented.
7Foundational processes are implemented.
8All projects within the program should follow the defined processes.
9Additional processes based on challenges are considered.
10Processes are constantly reviewed for improvements in their maturity.
Step 10 — continuous review for maturity improvements — is the backbone of a living governance framework. This maps to AARs (After Action Reports) and retrospectives that the effective CoE slide also emphasizes.

Fed angle

Steps 1–2 (document challenges, assess maturity) before prescribing solutions is exactly the architect mindset the Fed expects. You don't parachute in a governance framework; you diagnose first. Use this 10-step sequence as your "how I would approach governance at a new organization" answer.

Page 14 · Governance Categories

The Four Governance Categories

The components of a governance program fall into four major categories.

Center of Excellence

A group of cross-functional experts responsible for setting best practices, governance, and standards across the organization.

Change Management

The process of managing change within the overall program or project lifecycle, from collecting requirements through go-live in production.

Org Strategy

Refers to the design and structure of the foundational orgs that contain Salesforce applications.

Technical Governance

Covers the guiding principles used to develop the technical aspects of Salesforce.

Interview angle

When asked "what does a governance framework consist of?" this four-part answer — CoE, Change Management, Org Strategy, Technical Governance — is the canonical Salesforce response. Memorize the quad.

Page 15 · Governance Requirements

Governance Requirements

Governance ensures that the organization and Center of Excellence are aligned for the success of Salesforce implementations.

Clear Goal

Governance requires a clear goal or vision for the organization and the use of Salesforce, which can be referred to throughout the decision-making process.

Key Aspects

Governance involves assembling the right team, defining vision and strategies, and implementing the best practices for managing a Salesforce instance.

Page 16 · Governance Team

Governance Team

The success of a governance process relies on assembling the right team.

  • The size of the governance team can vary from a small group of a few individuals to a team of up to 10 members, depending on the organization's size and needs.
  • Key stakeholders — like the Executive Director, CEO, Directors of Business Units, technical staff, business owners, and end users — should be included in the Center of Excellence.
  • Inclusivity is crucial; the aim should be to build a diverse team. The Salesforce Administrator is an essential team member responsible for implementing governance decisions.

Fed angle

At the Fed, "assembling the right team" means including Compliance, InfoSec, and Risk Management as governance stakeholders — not just IT and business owners. Explicitly naming those non-traditional participants shows you understand regulated-environment governance.

Page 17 · Design Standards

Design Standards

Clear and compatible design standards associated with the vision can be defined.

Naming Conventions

Clear naming conventions should be defined. For example, all custom objects, fields, and classes can be appended with a 3-digit code of the business unit that made the request.

Description Fields

Use of description fields should be clearly defined. For example, all description fields must contain the name of the business unit that requested it and contain links to dependencies.

Apex Annotations

Custom Apex classes and triggers should be annotated with plain English explanations of logic. For example, a trigger can include what it does when a certain action occurs.

Design Documentation

Design documentation should use a standard template and include at least one image. It should be easily accessible, and regular audits must be performed to ensure compliance.

Fed angle

Regular audits of documentation for compliance is a direct parallel to OCC/Fed examination requirements. Plain-English code annotations and standardized design templates mean any examiner or auditor can understand what a component does without needing to decipher code — this is auditability by design.

Page 18 · Release and Deployment Strategy

Release and Deployment Strategy

An internal release strategy should ensure that releases are compatible with new core features. A deployment strategy defines how to create, test, and deploy changes.

Consistent Release Cadence

A consistent release strategy allows communicating with users promptly and effectively and ensures that sandboxes are created or refreshed in time for pre-release versions.

Trust & Adoption

Consistent internal releases build trust and foster the adoption of Salesforce. Release communication should include an area that thanks users who contributed ideas for enhancements.

Deployment Lifecycle

Changes should be developed and tested in a sandbox and deployed safely. A typical deployment lifecycle consists of stages such as development, QA, UAT, staging, and production.

Page 19 · Backlog Prioritization

Backlog Prioritization

In larger organizations, the Center of Excellence should meet regularly to prioritize the backlog of enhancements and feature requests.

Strategy

The group's understanding of each business unit and the organizational vision guides the strategic sequencing of projects.

Requests

The use of transparent systems like Jira, Confluence, or Slack is suggested for managing requests and fostering user engagement.

Alignment

Publicizing decision-making processes and project timelines helps align end-users with the organization's vision and set clear expectations.

Interview angle

The Fed uses Jira-style tooling for work tracking. Say that you've used transparent backlog tools to surface the CoE's prioritization logic — not just "we decided internally" but publicly posted so teams can see the rationale. Transparency in prioritization reduces shadow IT and unsanctioned changes.

Center Of Excellence

Page 21 · Need for CoE — Implementation & Challenges

Need for a Center of Excellence

The need for a Center of Excellence (CoE) is driven by many factors, including the size, ambition, and Salesforce footprint.

Implementation

CoE ensures that a Salesforce implementation is agile, running efficiently, and meets the needs of the staff.

Challenges

CoE can help deal with challenges such as running over budget, issues convincing users to adopt new functions, challenges working with multiple partners, falling behind schedule due to lack of decision-making, etc.

Page 22 · Need for CoE — Vision & Balance

Need for a CoE — Vision & Balance

Vision

The need for CoE should be driven by the organization's vision. The vision directly impacts how a Salesforce instance is used and its success. It can be used only as a CRM or for all the features and functions required by an organization.

Balance

It ensures that the divergent and convergent views and requirements are balanced against the available timelines and resources.

Page 23 · Need for CoE — Processes & Growth

Need for a CoE — Processes & Growth & Risk

Processes

A CoE streamlines organizational processes, selecting best practices from across multiple groups and removing inefficiencies and methods that have become outdated.

Growth & Risk

Business and technology experts on the CoE can help examine the impact of growth and ensure readiness to expand Salesforce usage. A CoE also ensures that the decisions made are the least risky possible. It balances a wide range of opinions and approaches and mitigates the loss of subject matter experts.

Fed angle

"Mitigates the loss of subject matter experts" is a real Fed concern — regulatory expertise in personnel is a key risk. A CoE that captures institutional knowledge in documented standards, patterns, and a program charter reduces key-person dependency — important in any organization where departures can leave critical knowledge gaps.

Page 24 · Center of Excellence Models

Center of Excellence Models

One of three CoE models or a hybrid can be utilized for a Center of Excellence.

Consolidated

Teams work in a single platform with a shared approach to data, constituents, and requirements for a unified CRM experience.

Federated

A federated model allows for independent group operation while maintaining a unified approach to CRM data.

Confederated

In a confederated model, each group may operate independently, potentially using separate Salesforce instances.

Interview angle

Federated CoE is the most common answer for large regulated enterprises with multiple business lines: independent execution within a shared governance umbrella. Be prepared to argue why Confederated (separate orgs) is sometimes necessary for data-residency or regulatory boundary reasons — relevant for the Fed's regional structure.

Page 25 · Building a Center of Excellence

Building a Center of Excellence

Various aspects need to be considered for building a Center of Excellence (CoE).

People & Roles

A CoE is made of people. Salesforce users engage with the CoE. A select subset of these users engage with defined roles.

Charter

A CoE is guided by a charter and manifests in meetings.

Output

A CoE provides output in the form of critical decisions and other deliverables.

Page 26 · People & Roles — Main Roles

People & Roles — Main Roles

A CoE involves various roles, each critical to its success. Selecting the right individuals for these roles is crucial.

Main Roles

Roles include Executive Sponsors, Program Owners, and various Steering Committees focusing on Business, Architecture, Release Management, Change Management, and Support.

Responsibilities

Each role has specific responsibilities, like defining organizational strategies, managing program execution, ensuring compliance with architectural standards, overseeing release processes, and leading change management initiatives.

Fed angle

The Architecture Steering Committee and the Release Management Steering Committee are the two bodies that would gate every change at the Fed. The Arch SC ensures designs comply with security and enterprise standards; the Release Management SC enforces the change calendar and deployment checklist — effectively the Change Advisory Board (CAB) in ITIL terms.

Page 27 · People & Roles — Additional Roles

People & Roles — Additional Roles

Additional Roles

Additional roles like Subject Matter Experts, Salesforce Power Users, User Group Members, and Business Users engage in specific ways, providing insights, feedback, and acting as liaisons to various committees.

Subject Matter Experts and Power Users are the field intelligence layer — they surface real problems back to the Steering Committees. Without this feedback loop, governance decisions are made without ground truth.
Page 28 · Program Charter — Key Members & Decision-Making

Program Charter

The program charter should define and contextualize CoE roles, including additional responsibilities related to the organization's mission.

Key Members

Key members should be identified before drafting the CoE charter, as they will shape its structure and function.

Decision-Making

Decision-making authority should be clearly defined at various levels (Project, Committee, Program Management/Steering Committee, Executive Sponsors) within the CoE.

Interview angle

Explicitly tiered decision authority — who can decide what at which level — is a critical governance design element. Exam questions often present scenarios where "who approves this change?" The answer flows from the charter's decision-authority matrix: small changes at the project level, large/risk-bearing changes escalate to Exec Sponsors.

Page 29 · Program Charter — Meeting Cadence & Well-Defined Charter

Program Charter — Meeting Cadence & Well-Defined Charter

Meeting Cadence

A regular meeting cadence should be established to ensure timely decision-making and communication with stakeholders.

Well-Defined Charter

A well-defined charter guides the CoE's work, clarifies complex decisions, and justifies resource allocation and decision-making processes.

Page 30 · Meetings — Types & Program Management

CoE Meetings

Regular meetings are essential for the effectiveness of the CoE, with their frequency outlined in the Program Charter.

Meeting Types

Different types include Program Management/Steering Committee Engagement, Executive Sponsor Engagement, and CoE Focus Areas meetings.

Program Management

Program Management meetings involve key decision-makers to ensure alignment on CoE focus areas, with biweekly 1-hour sessions.

Page 31 · Meetings — Executive Sponsor & Focus Areas

CoE Meetings — Executive Sponsor & Focus Areas

Executive Sponsor

Executive Sponsor meetings aim to update on project status and address key risks or decisions, initially lasting 1 hour and then reducing to 30 minutes monthly.

Focus Areas

Focus Areas meetings are conducted by Steering Committee members to ensure alignment across development workstreams, with participation depending on agenda needs.

Page 32 · Decisions & Other Deliverables

Decisions & Other Deliverables

The CoE aims for swift decision-making to leverage Salesforce effectively towards organizational goals.

Crucial Decisions

A CoE facilitates crucial decisions for implementation teams' progress, with some decisions — like funding or technical issues — requiring input from Executive Sponsors or specific Focus Areas.

Deliverables

Beyond decisions, CoE is responsible for deliverables such as technical standards, communication plans, and usage policies, with the Program Management/Steering Committee updating the Program Charter as needed.

Fed angle

Technical standards, communication plans, and usage policies as formal CoE deliverables are exactly what the Fed's Platform Engineer is expected to produce and maintain. These are the artifacts that make governance tangible and auditable — a regulator can ask to see them.

Page 33 · CoE Growth

CoE Growth

A CoE doesn't need to be complex initially; it can be simple first and expanded as needed.

Early Establishment

Early establishment of a CoE allows for better direction of its and Salesforce's program growth.

Steering Committee

Starting with a Steering Committee focused on the project and the consideration of Focus Areas provides long-term benefits.

Start simple, scale intentionally. A two-person Steering Committee with a one-page charter beats an elaborate governance structure that nobody follows. The exam frequently contrasts appropriate-weight governance vs. over-engineered governance.
Page 34 · Running a Center of Excellence

Running a Center of Excellence

To effectively run a Center of Excellence (CoE), a clear agenda should be established for various types of meetings, ensuring efficient communication and decision-making.

Logistics

Key elements include logistics planning for participant inclusivity, focusing on program health, updating action items, and reviewing project statuses to maintain transparency.

Discussions

Discussions should primarily revolve around addressing identified issues, requiring precise problem definition and prioritization.

Recapping Actions

Recapping actions ensures clarity on outcomes, while fostering an environment where questions are encouraged enhances understanding and engagement.

Continuous Involvement

Facilitating continuous CoE involvement outside meetings through collaborative tools like Chatter promotes a transparent and open culture.

Page 35 · Effective Center of Excellence

Effective Center of Excellence

Various factors should be considered to determine whether a Center of Excellence (CoE) is functioning effectively.

Progress Monitoring

Project progress should be monitored. Internal and external roadblocks should be addressed for on-time delivery.

Project Success

Project success should be measured by its adoption, influenced by change management and vision execution.

Budget Adherence

CoE should track budget adherence and facilitate decisions on budget changes.

Future-Proof Solutions

The aim should be for solutions that are future-proof, minimizing the need for redesigns and focusing on innovation.

AARs

AARs (After Action Reports) can be used to learn from each project stage, identifying what worked well or didn't.

Interview angle

AARs after each project stage are a maturity signal — a Stage 4 (Leading) governance practice. When you describe continuous improvement at your current org, framing it as "we ran a retro/AAR after each sprint and fed findings back to the CoE" positions you at the highest maturity level.

Change Management

Page 37 · Change Management

Change Management

Change Management refers to the process used by an organization to identify, prioritize, assign, execute and communicate changes.

Types of Changes

In the Salesforce deployment context, a change could be organizational, a business process change, addition or subtraction of a process, or a new version release by Salesforce.

Examples by Size

Big: rolling out a new business line. Medium: enhancements that affect a specific line of business. Small: fixes, config changes, reports, and templates.

Fed angle

At the Federal Reserve, even "small" changes (config, reports) go through a formal change request process. The Change Control Board categorizes changes by risk, not just size. A config change that affects security or data access is "small" in effort but "high risk" in impact — the CCB determines which gate applies.

Page 38 · Change Management Process — Initiate/Plan

Change Management Process — Initiate/Plan

Change Management is a process of continuous evolution that involves continuously analyzing the current state, collecting user feedback, and implementing change when appropriate.

Change management requires defining a vision, strategy, and objectives. The change management process includes three steps: Initiate/Plan, Operationalize, and Validate.

Initiate / Plan

In this stage, key Salesforce capabilities are identified, a roadmap to implement is developed, and capabilities are tied to program objectives.

Page 39 · Change Management Process — Operationalize & Validate

Change Management Process — Operationalize & Validate

Operationalize

In this phase, the application is built, configured, and deployed, organizational change is managed, and adoption of new features is driven.

Validate

In this stage, Salesforce data is audited, performance metrics are monitored, and results are used to drive behavior or process change within the organization where appropriate.

Fed angle

The Validate stage — data audited, metrics monitored — is the heart of change management in a regulated environment. Every deployed change should have a defined validation checklist, and results feed back to the CCB and CoE to confirm the change achieved its intended outcome and introduced no unintended risk.

Page 40 · Key Users

Key Users in Change Management

Key users who should be involved in a change management process are the user community, key decision makers, change control board, and executive sponsor.

User Community

Customer facing personnel, management, and senior management provide feedback and influence direction.

Key Decision Makers

An individual or group with proper training and understanding of the system, such as an enterprise architect, triages the feedback and requests from the field.

Change Control Board (CCB)

The Change Control Board (CCB) or Steering Committee is a cross-departmental group that meets regularly to review and approve change requests.

Executive Sponsor

The Executive Sponsor is the leader of the Steering Committee who sets priorities and ensures alignment with the overall business goals.

Interview angle

The CCB/Steering Committee is the canonical "who approves changes to production" answer. Be prepared to describe its cadence (weekly? bi-weekly?), what inputs it receives (change requests, risk assessments, deployment packages), and what it outputs (approval/rejection, conditions, emergency change expedite process).

Page 41 · Effective Change Management (Steps 1–5)

Effective Change Management — Steps 1–5

The following steps can be utilized for effective change management:

StepAction
1An execution strategy that is as simple as possible is defined.
2End users' input is collected, and interaction with the change management group is cultivated.
3An executive sponsor is engaged to establish objectives, define processes, and drive adoption.
4The level of effort, scope, and impact are determined, and processes between functional areas are identified and aligned.
5End users' input is obtained to understand the overall impact and prioritize user requirements.
Page 42 · Effective Change Management (Steps 6–9)

Effective Change Management — Steps 6–9

StepAction
6Implementation, end user validation, and deployment plan are defined.
7User community is informed of changes so that they are better prepared for adoption.
8A repeatable change management process is defined and adhered to.
9Power users are identified to follow up on the new release and define future enhancements.
Step 8 — repeatable process — is the key exam word. Governance processes must be repeatable and consistent, not ad hoc. A one-time checklist is not governance; a process that every change follows is.

Fed angle

Steps 6–9 describe the full deployment communication playbook: validate with end users, communicate proactively, repeat the process, identify advocates. At the Fed, Step 7 (inform the user community) means written release notes distributed before the change window — not a surprise. Step 8's "repeatable process" is what makes changes auditable: same steps, same evidence, every time.

Page 44 · Business Scenario

Business Scenario — Cosmic Innovation

Scenario

Cosmic Innovation, renowned for its smart home solutions, operates through two distinct divisions in North America and Europe, each utilizing its own Salesforce instance for operational processes. Facing challenges in change management, backlog management, design, and deployment planning due to varying governance models, the company seeks a Salesforce architect's expertise.

The architect's task is to assess the current governance structures and recommend enhancements, aiming to improve governance frameworks and lifecycle management practices used by the two divisions, streamline project delivery, and align it with the company's strategic goals for enhanced efficiency.

Interview angle

Two-org, multi-divisional scenario with varying governance models = the Fed's regional/functional structure. This is exactly the architect problem you'd face at a Fed where different business lines (Supervision, Research, Cash Operations, etc.) have autonomy but share a Salesforce platform. Your instinct: assess maturity per division, then recommend a federated CoE model with shared standards but divisional steering committees.

Requirements & Solutions

Page 46 · Requirement 1

Requirement 1 — North American Division

Scenario

The North American division is grappling with certain governance challenges, leading to inefficiencies in project delivery. Recent compliance changes have necessitated a 25% budget increase for a critical project, revealing the need for a structured governance framework to manage priorities and resource allocation. The backlog is overwhelmed with low-effort yet valuable enhancements, and there are often stakeholder disagreements. In addition, upcoming major deployments lack a cohesive strategy to ensure smooth transitions to live environments.

Page 47 · Solution 1 (Narrative)

Solution 1 — North American Division

Solution

The formation of a Center of Excellence, including Executive Sponsors and the Program Team, would standardize governance, enhance project alignment, and streamline delivery processes. A Change Control Board should authorize the additional budget and oversee any other change requests. To address backlog and stakeholder disagreements, facilitating the creation of a prioritization model with stakeholders is crucial. A citizen development program would address low-effort backlog items efficiently. For deployment readiness, all changes must be in source control, and the deployment package must be validated against production.

Fed angle

The CCB authorizing a 25% budget increase is the key governance moment here: no unilateral budget changes. The Fed has strict budget governance — a Change Control Board that owns both the technical approval and the budget authorization is the correct institutional model. Citizen development programs (low-code citizen developers) need guardrails — governance of who can build what, reviewed by the CoE.

Page 48 · Solution 1 (Summary)

Solution 1 — Summary Components

Center of Excellence

Standardizes governance and enhances project alignment.

Change Control Board

Authorizes budget changes and oversees all change requests.

Prioritization Model

Facilitates stakeholder agreement on backlog sequencing.

Citizen Development Program

Addresses low-effort backlog items efficiently.

Source Control

All changes in source control; deployment package validated against production.

Page 49 · Requirement 2

Requirement 2 — European Division

Scenario

The company's European division is confronting unique challenges in integrating multiple new projects within their Salesforce Org. There's a need to manage and prioritize a backlog of new feature requests that align with their strategic objectives. Additionally, the division faces design conflicts among project teams and the necessity to implement new custom Salesforce objects and enhanced security measures like SAML for Single Sign-On. Effective documentation and governance of these processes are also critical concerns.

Page 50 · Solution 2 (Part 1)

Solution 2 — European Division (Part 1)

Solution

Establishing a Center of Excellence (CoE) focused on European operations is essential. This CoE should create a charter document to streamline new project integrations within their Salesforce Org. It should also use a project management tool for backlog prioritization, addressing the influx of new feature requests. To prevent design conflicts, the CoE should develop design standards that all project teams must adhere to. Involving an Architecture Review Board would be beneficial for making technical decisions like implementing new custom objects and SAML for Single Sign-On.

Interview angle

The Architecture Review Board (ARB) is the technical authority body that the CoE calls on for complex technical decisions. Know the difference: CoE = program-level governance; ARB = technical design authority. For the Fed, the ARB would review any new integration pattern, security configuration (SAML, MFA enforcement, connected app settings), or data model change before it reaches production.

Page 51 · Solution 2 (Part 2)

Solution 2 — European Division (Part 2)

Solution

Additionally, robust documentation practices, including a documentation repository, change control log, and effective requirements management, should be integral components of the governance framework to ensure clear communication and alignment of all projects with the division's strategic goals.

Fed angle

A change control log is the audit trail that examiners will ask to see. Every change — who requested it, who approved it, what was deployed, when, and what the validation result was — must be traceable. The documentation repository ensures institutional memory survives staff turnover and supports examination readiness. These are not nice-to-haves; they are governance infrastructure.

Page 52 · Solution 2 (Part 3 — Summary)

Solution 2 — Summary Components

Center of Excellence

Focused on European operations with a formal charter document.

Project Management Tool

For backlog prioritization and alignment to strategic objectives.

Design Standards

Common standards all project teams must follow to prevent design conflicts.

Cheatsheet

Governance — the one-sentence definition

Governance is the minimal set of processes needed to deliver business outcomes with required quality within the business timeframe.

Four governance stages of maturity

StageNameSignal
1Non-Existent / BasicChanges directly in prod, no standards, no source control
2EmergingSome processes, inconsistently applied
3PracticingCoE established, charter, consistent processes, source control
4LeadingARB, automated quality gates, AARs, continuous improvement

Four governance program categories (memorize the quad)

  • Center of Excellence — cross-functional experts setting standards, best practices, governance.
  • Change Management — process to identify, prioritize, assign, execute, communicate changes. CCB reviews and approves.
  • Org Strategy — design and structure of foundational Salesforce orgs.
  • Technical Governance — guiding principles for technical aspects of Salesforce.

Three CoE models

ModelStructureUse when
ConsolidatedSingle platform, shared data/requirementsOne business, unified CRM
FederatedIndependent groups, unified CRM data approachMultiple business lines, shared org
ConfederatedEach group fully independent, separate orgs possibleDistinct regulatory/data-residency boundaries

CoE building blocks

  • People & Roles: Executive Sponsors, Program Owners, Steering Committees (Business / Architecture / Release / Change / Support), SMEs, Power Users.
  • Program Charter: defines roles, decision authority at all levels (Project → Committee → Steering → Exec Sponsor), meeting cadence.
  • Deliverables: technical standards, communication plans, usage policies, design standards, updated Program Charter.
  • Effectiveness indicators: adoption, budget adherence, on-time delivery, future-proof solutions, AARs after each stage.

Change management 3-phase process

PhaseWhat happens
Initiate / PlanIdentify Salesforce capabilities, build roadmap, tie to program objectives
OperationalizeBuild, configure, deploy; manage org change; drive adoption
ValidateAudit data, monitor metrics, use results to drive behavior / process change

Change management key roles

  • User Community — provides feedback, influences direction.
  • Key Decision Maker (e.g. enterprise architect) — triages field feedback.
  • Change Control Board (CCB) / Steering Committee — cross-departmental, reviews and approves change requests.
  • Executive Sponsor — leads Steering Committee, sets priorities, ensures business alignment.

10-step governance framework build

  1. Document delivery challenges.
  2. Understand maturity in core framework processes.
  3. Define clear vision and strategy; align stakeholders.
  4. Business owns and prioritizes requirements.
  5. Review current data architecture and management.
  6. Implement basic principles of software engineering.
  7. Implement foundational processes.
  8. All projects follow defined processes.
  9. Consider additional processes for specific challenges.
  10. Constantly review processes for maturity improvements.

Scenario solution patterns (exam traps)

SymptomGovernance response
Budget overruns / uncontrolled scopeChange Control Board (CCB) to authorize all budget/scope changes
Backlog overwhelmed, stakeholder disagreementsCoE + prioritization model with stakeholders + citizen development program for low-effort items
Design conflicts between teamsCoE design standards + Architecture Review Board (ARB)
No audit trail, documentation gapsDocumentation repository + change control log + requirements management
Multiple divisions, inconsistent governanceFederated CoE model with shared charter but divisional steering committees
Complex technical decisions (SAML, custom objects)Architecture Review Board

Key vocabulary distinctions

  • CoE = the body; Program Charter = the document that defines it.
  • CCB = approves changes; ARB = approves designs/architecture.
  • Governance = minimal processes; not maximum bureaucracy — right-size to maturity.
  • Federated = one org, independent groups; Confederated = separate orgs.
  • Citizen development program = governed low-code channel for admin-level users to address backlog.
