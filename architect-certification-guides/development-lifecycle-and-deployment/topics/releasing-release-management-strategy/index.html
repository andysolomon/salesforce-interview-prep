Page 4 · Introduction

Introduction

In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, developing a robust release management strategy tailored to specific customer scenarios is crucial for the integrity and efficiency of Salesforce projects. This approach involves analyzing the customer's operational requirements, development processes, and business goals to devise a strategic plan that ensures smooth, consistent, and successful deployment of Salesforce updates.

By incorporating different types of releases (immediate, minor, major, and hotfix) in their release management strategy, organizations can significantly enhance the delivery of new functionality on their Salesforce platform. An effective release management strategy aligns releases with business objectives, facilitating seamless integration of new features and improvements.

Fed angle

A regulated central bank needs a formal, documented release management strategy precisely because ad-hoc deployments create untrackable change events. The four release types (immediate, minor, major, hotfix) map directly to a change-control tiering model: low-risk same-day changes, scheduled monthly changes, quarterly platform-aligned releases, and emergency break-fix procedures — each with corresponding CAB/approval gates.

Page 7 · Release Management — Process & Pillars

Release Management

Release Management is the continuous process that comprises specific stages that should be followed for every successful release.

Process

Typically, release management comprises planning, requirement gathering, solution design, build, test, release, and maintenance.

Steps

Each step of the release management process is essential and should be fully completed before moving onto the next step.

Pillars

The main pillars of release management are deployment size and cadence, new project evaluation, environment strategy, training and communication, and ongoing support.

Interview angle

When asked to describe release management, use the three-tier answer: process (the sequential stages), steps (each must be fully completed before advancing), and pillars (the five structural dimensions). This demonstrates architect-level thinking rather than just enumerating deployment steps. The "fully completed before moving on" principle is a key exam gotcha — skipping steps is a root cause of production incidents.

Page 8 · Release Management — Planning & Evaluation

Release Management

Release Management is the continuous process that comprises specific stages that should be followed for every successful release.

Release Planning

Release planning involves understanding the components being developed, anticipating potential conflicts, and scheduling features for each release.

Release Schedule

A release schedule should be established for a project. Scheduled release milestones should be added to the project schedule to aid planning.

Project Evaluation

Project evaluation involves submission of a project request to the COE (Center Of Excellence), evaluation of short term and long term alignment with the goals, project prioritization, and project decision.

Fed angle

At a regulated institution, the release schedule is not just a project management artifact — it is a control document. Milestones in the schedule become the checkpoints at which change-advisory board (CAB) sign-off is required. COE evaluation of short-term vs. long-term alignment is directly analogous to the Fed's IT governance model, where all significant technology changes must demonstrate alignment with the strategic roadmap before approval.

Page 9 · New Project Evaluation Steps

Release Management — New Project Evaluation

Evaluation of new projects in the release management process consist of multiple steps.

1. New Project Submission

A communication process is defined for the new project, the project request is submitted to the COE, and project information is entered into the database.

2. Long Term Evaluation / Support

The COE evaluates the short term and long term alignment with the goals, alignment with ongoing or other pending projects, how project will be supported, and administrative needs.

3. Project Prioritization

New project requests are reviewed as part of a COE meeting, business priority and alignment with the overall program roadmap are determined, and priorities are defined in the COE project database.

4. Project Decision

The COE advises on project strategy and timing, informs the Steering Committee about upcoming projects, and communicates decision to sponsors. Relevant individuals join the COE for advancing projects.

Page 10 · Types of Releases — Overview

Types of Releases

There are three main types of releases that should be considered while devising a release management strategy.

Immediate

Immediate releases involve small changes that are often rapidly applied directly in production. Their impact on users is minimal, and they are outside the scope of change process.

Minor

Minor releases involve minor changes that impact two or more groups of users. They are thrice as often as a major release and have minor impact on training and production.

Major

Major releases involve significant changes and are often aligned with platform releases. They have major impact on production and integration across more than one business unit.

There are three main release types. Hotfixes are a separate, fourth category used only to resolve urgent production issues — they are not a regular scheduled release type.
Page 11 · Types of Releases — Effort & Resources

Types of Releases

There are three main types of releases that should be considered while devising a release management strategy.

Immediate

Immediate releases require low level of effort, no training, and no integration. They can be implemented by a BA (Business Analyst).

Minor

Minor releases require medium level of effort, less than one day of training, and less than one week of configuration time. Their implementation requires IT involvement.

Major

Major releases require high level of effort, more than one day of training, more than one week of configuration time, more than one week of integration development time, and IT Lead's involvement.

Interview angle

The effort thresholds are high-yield exam content: BA handles immediate, IT team handles minor, IT Lead is required for major. Memorize the breakpoints — less than one day / less than one week separates minor from major. These thresholds also determine which approval path applies, making them crucial for classifying real project work.

Page 12 · Types of Releases — Cadence & Examples

Types of Releases

There are three main types of releases that should be considered while devising a release management strategy.

Immediate

Immediate releases are often implemented daily. Examples include reports, dashboards, related lists, data loads, territory assignments, etc.

Minor

Minor releases are often implemented monthly. Examples include new objects, fields, page layouts, flows, triggers, etc.

Major

Major releases are often implemented quarterly. Examples include AppExchange apps, process impacting changes, data migration, integration changes, etc.

Fed angle

The daily/monthly/quarterly cadence aligns naturally with a regulated institution's change-control calendar. Immediate (daily) changes bypass formal CAB because they are low-risk; minor (monthly) changes go through standard CAB review; major (quarterly) changes require full change-advisory board approval with pre-deployment testing sign-off and a rollback plan. Packaging these into versioned unlocked packages makes each deployment traceable and repeatable for audit purposes.

Page 13 · Hotfixes

Hotfixes

A hotfix is a small change that is applied to resolve an urgent production issue.

Quick Rollout

Hotfixes have a fast rollout path to address a burning production issue. The release path is typically defined at the time of evaluating the required change.

UAT

User acceptance testing (UAT) may or may not be required, depending on the nature of change. The hotfix could be directly applied in production.

Downstream

The deployment should flow through all downstream environments, ensuring that the change is replicated to the other environments.

After a hotfix is applied to production, it must flow downstream to all other environments to prevent environment drift. Skipping this step is a common mistake that causes sandbox environments to become out of sync with production.
Page 14 · Release Management Groups

Release Management Groups

Two groups are responsible for the creation and execution of release management processes.

Steering Committee

The Steering Committee provides strategic direction and oversight. It typically consists of senior stakeholders whose role involves decision-making on priorities, resource allocation, and addressing any conflicts or changes in direction, ensuring that the release management processes support the organization's objectives.

Center Of Excellence (COE)

The Center of Excellence (COE) plays a pivotal role in standardizing and overseeing the execution of release management processes. It establishes best practices, methodologies, and guidelines to be followed. It acts as a governing body that works closely with development teams to ensure consistency, efficiency, and quality.

Interview angle

The Steering Committee vs. COE distinction is a common exam scenario. The Steering Committee is the executive oversight body (strategic direction, priority decisions, conflict resolution). The COE is the operational body (best practices, standards, execution oversight). They are complementary: the Steering Committee sets the "what and when," the COE owns the "how." Knowing which group handles which type of decision is key to answering scenario questions correctly.

Page 15 · Release Paths

Release Paths

The diagram below shows the sample release paths that may be followed for different types of releases in Salesforce.

Release TypeRelease Path
Immediate Release Development → Quality Assurance (QA) → Production
Minor Release Development → Quality Assurance (QA) → UAT → Production
Major Release Development → Quality Assurance (QA) → Integration Testing → UAT → Production
Hotfix UAT (Optional) → Production
The major release path is the only one that includes an Integration Testing stage before UAT. This reflects the cross-business-unit impact of major releases, which require verification that integrations still function correctly before broader user acceptance testing.

Fed angle

In a regulated deployment pipeline (e.g., GitLab CI/CD), these release paths map directly to pipeline stage gates: each arrow is a checkpoint requiring a passing test suite or a human approval before the next stage runs. For a central bank, the major release path — with its explicit Integration Testing stage — satisfies the segregation-of-duties requirement that no single team can promote a change directly from dev to UAT without an independent integration validation step.

Business Scenario — Cosmic Manufacturing Solutions

Read the business scenario below and determine the most appropriate solutions for the company's requirements.

Scenario

Cosmic Manufacturing Solutions, a leader in the industrial sector, is set to enhance its Salesforce platform, focusing on Sales Cloud and Service Cloud enhancements. These updates aim to optimize the sales pipeline management, improve customer service processes, and ensure seamless integration with its manufacturing operations system. Projects include customizing Sales Cloud to improve tracking of sales opportunities and leveraging Service Cloud to enhance post-sale support and service request tracking.

The company's Salesforce architect faces the task of devising release management strategies for the development and deployment of the enhancements. The strategies must ensure that the enhancements are implemented effectively, improving operational efficiency and customer satisfaction without disrupting ongoing sales and service activities.

Page 19 · Requirement 1 (Part 1)

Requirement 1 (Part 1)

Scenario

The company has well-defined release management processes for both large and small projects. However, its Salesforce development teams are facing some challenges and have the following requirements related to release management:

  1. The company has seasonal business operations and approaching its busiest season. A development team has been working on a major Sales Cloud project. The Team Lead requires advice about releasing it in production, considering the impact on sales operations.
Page 20 · Requirement 1 (Part 2)

Requirement 1 (Part 2)

Scenario

  1. A developer has created an Apex trigger and a record-triggered flow on the Opportunity object for a requirement of the sales director. The architect needs to recommend an approach for the release planning of these changes.
  2. Moving forward, the CTO would like the release management processes to be created and executed by specific user groups for better control and management. The architect needs to provide an appropriate recommendation.
Page 21 · Solution 1 (Part 1)

Solution 1 (Part 1)

Solution

  1. The company should avoid launching the major project in production near peak business periods to ensure that the impact on sales operations is minimal.
  2. The release of the new Apex trigger and record-triggered flow should be planned as a minor release with training and change management practices. They represent minor changes that would not impact more than two or more groups of users and would have minor impact on training and production.
Classifying the Apex trigger + record-triggered flow as a minor release (not immediate) is correct because they impact the Opportunity object used by the sales director — spanning a specific user group — and require some training. If the change had no user impact and required no training, it would be classified as immediate.
Page 22 · Solution 1 (Part 2)

Solution 1 (Part 2)

Solution

  1. The Center Of Excellence (COE) and Steering Committee should be responsible for the creation and execution of release management processes. The Steering Committee provides strategic direction and oversight. The Center of Excellence (COE) plays a pivotal role in standardizing and overseeing the execution of release management processes.

Interview angle

This solution directly maps to the CTO's requirement for "specific user groups for better control." The canonical answer is always the COE + Steering Committee pair — not a single group alone. The Steering Committee owns governance direction; the COE owns execution standards. Together they provide the segregation of duties that regulated organizations require: the body setting priorities is different from the body enforcing process compliance.

Page 23 · Requirement 2

Requirement 2

Scenario

The company's CTO would like to develop a governance framework for release planning and has asked the architect for recommendations regarding decisions related to testing. In addition, one of the projects is in the testing phase, and the testing team has found several issues in the new functionality that will be deployed to production. As the project deadline is short, the team has proposed that the issues be fixed in the testing sandbox and then deployed to production. The architect needs to provide an appropriate recommendation.

Page 24 · Solution 2

Solution 2

Solution

In a governance framework for release planning, important decisions related to testing include the following:

  1. When should a new feature release be tested in the development lifecycle, especially when issues are expected?
  2. How should existing functionality be tested to ensure that no regressions are introduced?

The issues found by the testing team should be fixed in a Developer sandbox. The changes should then be migrated to the testing sandbox for thorough testing. Once testing is complete, they should be deployed to production.

The team's proposal to fix issues directly in the testing sandbox violates the principle of environment segregation. Fixes must always originate in the Developer sandbox, move to testing, and only then proceed to production. Fixing in the testing sandbox skips the development environment, which bypasses the full change lifecycle and risks contaminating the test environment.

Fed angle

Environment segregation — Developer sandbox → Testing sandbox → Production — is the software equivalent of segregation of duties. At a regulated institution, allowing fixes to be applied directly in the testing sandbox would violate the principle that no change enters production without traversing the defined promotion path. This is an auditable control: each migration event between environments should be logged with a ticket reference, deploying officer, and approver.

High-Yield Exam Gotchas

  • Three main release types, not four — Immediate, Minor, and Major are the three main types. Hotfix is a separate, urgent-issue-only category, not a regular release type.
  • Immediate releases are outside the scope of the change process — they are applied directly in production and do not require formal change management approval.
  • Minor = 3× more often than major — monthly vs. quarterly; minor releases are thrice as frequent as major releases.
  • Major releases require an Integration Testing stage that minor and immediate releases do not have in their release path.
  • Hotfix UAT is optional — the hotfix could be directly applied in production depending on the nature of the change. However, it must always flow downstream to all other environments afterward.
  • Never fix issues in the testing sandbox — fixes must always originate in the Developer sandbox, then migrate to testing, then to production.
  • COE ≠ Steering Committee — COE owns execution and standardization; Steering Committee owns strategic direction and oversight. Both are required for complete release governance.
  • Release schedule milestones belong in the project schedule — not just in a release-specific document. This integration ensures release planning is visible to the broader project team.
  • Avoid major releases near peak business periods — release timing must account for business operational calendars, not just technical readiness.

Fed angle — Tying it together

For a central bank platform engineer, release management strategy is a regulatory and audit obligation, not just a delivery best practice. The four release types map to a tiered change-control model (routine / standard / significant / emergency). The COE + Steering Committee governance structure directly satisfies the segregation-of-duties requirement that the body proposing changes is separate from the body approving them. The defined release paths (especially Major's Integration Testing gate) provide the audit trail that examiners and internal audit need to confirm that no untested code reaches production. Versioned unlocked packages or change sets tracked in a source control system add traceability: every production deployment has a commit hash, a deploying officer, and an approver on record.
