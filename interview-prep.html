Framework

STAR refresher

Behavioral answers should land in 90–120 seconds. The shape:

S — Situation

One sentence of business context. Who, where, why it mattered.

T — Task

Your specific responsibility. Name it explicitly — interviewers want to know what you owned.

A — Action

The longest part. Concrete steps you took, decisions you made, tradeoffs you weighed. Bring numbers.

R — Result

Quantified outcome + what you learned or would do differently.

Common mistakes

"We did X" → no, "I did X." Don't skip Result. Don't ramble through Situation. Pivot to Action within 20 seconds.

Framework

Your story anchors

The handful of projects you'll keep returning to. Memorize one quantified result for each.

VA case management Experience Cloud partner portal Bulk API + S3 ingestion pipeline 2GP pipeline (SFDX, GitHub Actions) Jest standards rollout Playwright E2E Apex/LWC perf optimization Mentoring & code review at scale

Interview reflex

Before any technical question, mentally ask: "which of my eight anchors gives me the cleanest narrative for this?" If two anchors fit, pick the one with the better quantified result.

Question 1

Tell me about yourself and why this Salesforce Tech Lead role at Kapitus interests you.

Likely follow-up: How does your Experience Cloud, Apex, LWC, and DevOps background map to a fintech lending platform?

What they're testing

  • Can you tell a coherent story arc — not a resume read-back?
  • Have you done specific homework on Kapitus (small-business financing, partner marketplace, regulated data)?
  • Tech Lead = hands-on coder + architecture owner + people leader. Show all three in 90 seconds.

Mapping to Kapitus (use these explicitly)

  • Experience Cloud → borrower portal, partner/broker portal, document upload & status tracking.
  • Apex + LWC → underwriting workflow UI, custom approval logic, real-time decisioning UX.
  • DevOps (SFDX + 2GP + GH Actions) → faster, safer releases in a regulated environment with audit trails.
  • Integrations → bank data, credit bureaus, fraud/KYC, partner marketplaces.

Close strong

"What pulls me to Kapitus specifically is the combination of partner-driven distribution and small-business credit decisioning — both areas where Salesforce can be either a liability or a strategic asset depending on how it's architected. That gap is exactly what I've spent the last few years closing."

Question 2

Walk me through a Salesforce system you designed end-to-end. What were the key architecture decisions?

Likely follow-up: Why not X (the alternative they'd pick)? What would you change about it now?

Lead with

Your VA case management, Experience Cloud portal, or Bulk API/S3 pipeline. Pick the one with the cleanest decision narrative — Tech Lead interviews are about how you choose, not what you ship.

Frame the answer around 3–4 decisions

  1. Data model — why master-detail vs lookup, where you used custom metadata vs custom settings, sharing model (OWD + role hierarchy + criteria-based vs Apex managed).
  2. Integration boundary — sync REST vs async Bulk vs Platform Events. Idempotency & replay.
  3. UX — LWC vs Flow vs Aura. Where you drew the "build vs configure" line.
  4. Release strategy — packages vs org dev, source tracking, who can deploy to prod.

The "what would you change" judo

Always have a credible answer. Best ones: "I'd move X from Flow to Apex because the volume crossed N records/day" — shows you understand thresholds, not just patterns.

Question 3

Design a Salesforce-based loan application intake system for small businesses.

Frame the business model first

Open with: "Most small-business lenders rely on broker/partner submission rather than direct consumer intake, so I'd design partner-API-first — but with a self-serve fallback for the long tail." This signals you understand how Kapitus actually books loans, which is worth more than the architecture diagram in this answer.

What they want to hear (in roughly this order)

  1. Channels (primary → secondary): Inbound partner/marketplace API (broker LOS, ISO networks), Partner Community for broker-assisted submission, agent-assisted (Sales Cloud) for white-glove deals, Experience Cloud public site for direct/self-serve as a smaller channel.
  2. Data model:
    • Account = Business; Contact = Principal/Guarantor (with multiple per business).
    • Lead or custom Application__c for intake → convert/related on qualification.
    • Opportunity for the funded loan; Product2 + PricebookEntry for loan products.
    • ContentDocument/ContentVersion for bank statements, tax returns, IDs.
  3. Routing: Lead Assignment Rules or Flow-driven routing keyed on state, product type, partner source. Round-robin queues for underwriters.
  4. Integrations: Plaid/Yodlee for bank data, Experian/Equifax for bureau, OFAC/sanctions, KYC/KYB.
  5. Security & compliance: field-level encryption, Shield Event Monitoring, separate persona-based permission sets, audit trail.
  6. Decisioning: Flow + Apex hybrid — declarative for business-policy edits, Apex for scoring logic with full test coverage.
  7. Observability: custom Application_Event__c for SLA timestamps; reports + dashboards for funnel conversion by partner.
Likely follow-up: How would you handle PII? How would you bulk-import an old book of business? What if a partner sends 100k apps in one hour?

Bring up before they ask

State limits and disclosures (lending varies by state). Idempotent API for partner submissions. Soft-decline vs hard-decline so brokers can re-submit.

Question 4

How would you integrate Salesforce with external underwriting, banking, or partner systems?

Cover the full matrix

Pattern

Sync REST · Async Bulk · Platform Events · Change Data Capture · MuleSoft/middleware

Auth

Named Credentials, OAuth 2.0 Client Credentials, JWT bearer for system-to-system

Reliability

Idempotency keys, retries w/ exponential backoff, dead-letter queue, circuit breaker

Observability

Integration_Log__c, Setup Audit Trail, Event Monitoring, OpenTelemetry via middleware

Specific Salesforce mechanics

  • HttpCalloutMock / WebServiceMock for unit tests — never let real HTTP into Apex tests.
  • Wrap callouts in Test.startTest()/stopTest() when DML precedes them, or hit the "uncommitted work" error.
  • Named Credentials + External Credentials for secret management — no API keys in Custom Settings.
  • For bursts: Bulk API 2.0 or Pub/Sub API (gRPC) rather than synchronous REST.
Likely follow-up: A partner is sending us 5x their normal traffic and our callout limit is being blown. What do you do?

Concrete answer to the follow-up

Short term: queueable + chained jobs to spread callouts across transactions; Platform Cache to deduplicate. Medium term: introduce middleware (MuleSoft or a Vercel Function) to absorb bursts and call SF asynchronously. Long term: renegotiate the partner contract for batched submission.

Question 5

Tell me about a time you improved Salesforce delivery or release quality.

Lead with your 2GP pipeline anchor

This is your sweet spot. Tell it as STAR.

  • S: Releases were taking N hours / failing rate of X% / no clear ownership.
  • T: You owned the platform engineering side — pipeline, conventions, enforcement.
  • A:
    • Moved to 2GP unlocked packages; established sfdx-project.json + dependency graph.
    • Set up GitHub Actions with stages: lint → Jest → Apex tests → validate-only deploy → integration.
    • Adopted Jest with Kent C. Dodds-style behavioral tests (no implementation details).
    • Added Playwright for E2E coverage of the highest-revenue user paths.
    • Wrote contributor docs + held office hours for the first 4 weeks.
  • R: Deploy time dropped from X to Y; failure rate from A% to B%; team shipped feature N two weeks early.
Likely follow-up: What did the team push back on? How did you handle it?

Be honest about adoption pain

Pretending everyone loved it on day one is a tell. Real answer: "Two senior engineers preferred change sets. I paired with them on the first three deploys and let them see the diff between source-driven and click-to-deploy — they converted themselves by week three."

Question 6

How do you approach Salesforce security and governance in a regulated or sensitive-data environment?

The Salesforce security stack — top to bottom

  1. Org-wide defaults → tightest reasonable (Private for sensitive objects).
  2. Role hierarchy → grants up the chain (managers see reports' records).
  3. Sharing rules → criteria-based or owner-based for broader access.
  4. Manual sharing → user-initiated; gone on owner change.
  5. Apex Managed Sharing → custom RowCause, only on custom objects, survives ownership changes.
  6. CRUD/FLS → enforced at object & field. Apex needs to respect WITH SECURITY_ENFORCED / Security.stripInaccessible() / USER_MODE.

Persona-based pattern (drop this in)

  • One permission set per persona (Underwriter, Servicer, Broker, Compliance).
  • Permission set groups for composite roles ("Senior Underwriter" = Underwriter + Servicer).
  • Persona-based Apex tests with System.runAs(user) for each persona — catches access bugs at deploy time.
  • Shield Platform Encryption on PII fields (SSN, EIN, DOB, bank account numbers).
  • Event Monitoring for audit; Setup Audit Trail for config changes; Field History Tracking for record-level history.
Likely follow-up: An audit found that brokers can see other brokers' applications. How do you find and fix it?

The diagnostic answer

"Query AccountShare/Application__Share for the affected records to see why they're shared — Owner, Manual, sharing rule name, or Apex custom RowCause. Most leaks I've seen come from criteria-based sharing rules with overly broad criteria, or from WITH SECURITY_ENFORCED being missed in a recent SOQL query. Then I'd add a System.runAs(brokerUser) test that asserts they can only see their own apps, so it never happens again."

Question 7

Describe a complex Apex or LWC performance issue you solved.

Likely follow-up: How did you prove the fix worked, and how did you prevent regressions?

Concrete frame

  • The symptom: page taking N seconds / SOQL row limit / heap exception / UNABLE_TO_LOCK_ROW.
  • The diagnostic tools:
    • Developer Console Execution Overview: Timeline for category breakdown, Limits tab for governor pressure, Executed Units for method call counts.
    • Query Plan tab to find unindexed selective queries.
    • Apex Replay Debugger for stepping through prod-like execution.
    • Browser DevTools for LWC: $0 on the component to inspect @wired state, Performance tab for renders.
  • The fix: bulkified for loop → single DML, denormalized field to skip a query, moved hot path to Platform Cache, deferred non-critical work to Queueable, replaced N+1 SOQL with subquery.
  • The proof: before/after numbers from the Executed Units tab; user-perceived load time from Lightning Usage App; regression-locked with a perf test in CI that asserts SOQL count and total time.

Sharpest answer

"The fix was easy — finding it took 80% of the time. The takeaway I push on the team: instrument first, optimize second. We now log query count + heap to a Perf_Log__c in the highest-traffic paths so we see degradation in dashboards, not in tickets."

Question 8

Tell me about a time you had to lead engineers while still staying hands-on.

What they're probing

  • Do you mentor, or just dictate?
  • Do you do code review well — substantive, kind, fast?
  • Do you delegate, or are you a bottleneck?
  • What do you reserve for yourself vs. push to the team?

Framing that lands

  1. "I keep my hands on the most-leveraged parts: the conventions, the platform pieces, the toughest architectural cuts."
  2. "I push features and incremental work to the team — pair when it's a teaching moment, review async otherwise."
  3. "I aim for a 4-hour code-review SLA. I'd rather be the fast reviewer than the perfect one — fast review keeps people unblocked, and 'perfect' often just means 'how I'd do it.'"
  4. "I run a weekly 30-minute office hour. No agenda. Most engineers don't come; the ones who do save me hours later."

Concrete anti-bottleneck mechanism

"For any non-trivial change, the engineer writes a one-page design doc before code. Two engineers review it. I review it. If we three agree, the design's done — I don't review the PR for architecture, just for the bar. That cut my PR-review queue from 30 → 6."

Question 10

If you joined Kapitus and inherited a messy Salesforce org with slow releases and unclear ownership, what would you do in your first 90 days?

Days 1–30 — Listen & measure

  • 1:1s with every engineer + key stakeholders (product, ops, compliance, support).
  • Read the last 3 months of post-mortems, support escalations, and deploy logs.
  • Inventory: number of triggers per object, Flow count, custom code coverage %, deploy frequency & failure rate, oldest scheduled jobs, top 10 slowest pages.
  • Run force:package:version:report --verbose on existing packages.
  • Identify the 3 things that are actively bleeding (production bugs, audit risks, broken integrations).

Days 31–60 — Stabilize

  • Land the bleeding fixes first — quickest path to credibility.
  • Stand up a basic CI gate: lint + tests + validate-only deploy. Even minimal CI catches the most common deploy failures.
  • Establish code review conventions and SLAs. Pair on the first few reviews to set the bar.
  • Draft a 6-month platform roadmap. Share with engineering + product, get buy-in.

Days 61–90 — Invest

  • Begin migration off worst tech debt (often: triggers without a framework, untested Apex, hardcoded IDs).
  • Move toward 2GP or unlocked packages if not already there. Start small — one module.
  • Define persona-based permission sets if missing; audit FLS coverage.
  • Set up dashboards for the metrics you'll be judged on: deploy time, failure rate, P1 incident count, code coverage trend.
  • Hand-off a piece of platform ownership to a strong IC — start growing the next Tech Lead.

What they're really testing

Patience + judgment. Junior leads come in swinging and break trust. Senior leads measure first, fix the bleeding, then invest. Show that distinction.

Deep-dive · Industry data model

Financial Services Cloud & Party Architecture

Non-negotiable for a fintech-lending Tech Lead loop. Walking in without FSC vocabulary signals "vanilla CRM only."

The model in one paragraph

FSC sits on top of Sales Cloud and reshapes the data model around parties (people + organizations + the relationships between them) rather than the standard Account/Contact dyad. The core add-ons are Person Accounts, Household, Financial Account, Asset/Liability, Goal, Card, and Loan. Industries Cloud (FSC included) layers Party / Party Relationship / Party Role objects for n-to-n party graphs — beneficial owners, guarantors, related entities — which standard Account hierarchies can't model cleanly.

Pills to drop

Person Accounts Household Financial Account Asset / Liability Party / Party Relationship Party Role Action Plans Rollups (Asset/Liability)

When you'd use FSC vs roll-your-own

  • FSC when you're greenfield and can take the licensing line item — you inherit Person Accounts, Household rollups, Action Plans, and a release cadence Salesforce maintains for you.
  • Custom Party model when the existing org has deep custom relationships and FSC's opinions would force a migration. Cheaper short term, more code to maintain.
  • For Kapitus specifically: small-business borrowers are usually organizations with multiple principals/guarantors — a Party Group graph rather than a single Account. FSC's Party Relationship object handles that natively; standard SF requires a custom junction with a sharing model attached.
Likely follow-up: Would you use FSC at Kapitus or build your own?

Sharp answer

"My default is FSC if the licensing fits the deal size — the Party model is exactly the shape a small-business lender needs, and skipping it means you'll rebuild Household and Asset/Liability over the next two years. The gating question is license cost per underwriter and whether the existing org is too entrenched in standard Accounts to migrate cleanly. I'd want to see your contact and license breakdown in week one before committing."

Deep-dive · Automation

Record-triggered Flows — BEFORE vs AFTER save

Every senior SF interview in 2026 probes this distinction. Most candidates fumble it because Salesforce changed the order of execution and the official docs are split across releases.

Side-by-side

BEFORE save

Fires before the DB write. Same-record field updates only — no DML, no callouts. Faster (no second save). No rollback from inside the flow. Replaces most workflow rules & simple before-insert triggers.

AFTER save

Fires after the DB write but before commit. Can do DML on other records, send Platform Events, call subflows, schedule async work. Use for related-record updates, callouts (async only), event emission.

Order of execution (the part that trips people up)

  1. System validation rules + load original record
  2. Before-save flows
  3. Apex before triggers
  4. Custom validation rules
  5. Record saved to DB (not yet committed)
  6. Apex after triggers
  7. After-save flows
  8. Assignment / auto-response / workflow rules
  9. Roll-up summary + parent recalc
  10. Commit

The gotcha: flow fires after Apex triggers in its phase, not before. Engineers assume "flow first" and then can't explain why their Apex saw a stale value.

Likely follow-up: Where would you put a field update that fires on every Opportunity save?

Sharp answer

"Before-save flow if it's same-record only — fastest path, no recursion risk, no Apex needed. I move to Apex trigger once it crosses cross-object logic, governor-sensitive bulk patterns (>200 records per transaction in a hot path), or anywhere the team will need to unit-test the path with mocks. Rule of thumb: declarative until volume or complexity forces the move."

Deep-dive · Async Apex

Future · Queueable · Batch · Schedulable

Tech Lead staple. Be ready for "I have N million records and a callout per record — how?"

The four flavors

@future

Stateless. No chaining. Only primitive params (no sObjects). Mostly legacy — prefer Queueable unless you literally need @future(callout=true) from a trigger context.

Queueable

Returns a Job ID. Accepts sObjects + complex types. Chainable (depth limit of 5 in tests; effectively unlimited in prod). Implements Database.AllowsCallouts for HTTP.

Batch

For >50M records. Scope size 1–2000 (default 200). start → execute → finish. No guaranteed order across batches. Heap resets per execute(). Stateful via Database.Stateful.

Schedulable

Cron-based via System.schedule(). 100 scheduled jobs per org ceiling. System.scheduleBatch() for one-shot fire-and-forget.

Critical patterns

  • Bulk + callouts: Batch with scope 200, implements Database.AllowsCallouts, finish-method chains a Queueable for cleanup. Track failures in Integration_Log__c via Database.saveResult.
  • Database.saveResult — Database.insert(records, false) returns per-record success/error without rolling back the whole transaction. Critical for partial-success ingestion pipelines.
  • Test.startTest() / stopTest() — forces all queued async work to run synchronously inside the block. Without it, async tests assert against unrun jobs.
  • No guaranteed order in batch — never write logic that depends on batch N finishing before batch M.
Likely follow-up: 5 million loans need a credit-bureau refresh tonight. Walk me through it.

Sharp answer

"Schedulable kicks off a Batch with scope 200, Database.AllowsCallouts on the batch class. Each execute() calls the bureau, writes results, and on partial-success writes the failures to Integration_Log__c via Database.saveResult. finish() chains a Queueable that emits a Platform Event (Bureau_Refresh_Complete__e) so downstream systems pick it up. Idempotency key on the bureau side so re-runs after partial failure are safe. Throttling: scope size tuned to stay under the partner's per-minute rate limit — not the SF callout limit."

Deep-dive · Event-driven

Platform Events · Change Data Capture · Pub/Sub API

For a fintech the integration matrix matters as much as the data model. Lending platforms generate enormous event streams — application created, document uploaded, decision rendered, loan funded, payment posted, payment missed.

The three mechanisms

Platform Events

You define them (Event__e). Subscribe from Apex, Flow, LWC empAPI, or external. Standard (3-day retention, durable) vs High-volume (1 day, best-effort). Publish via DML or Apex EventBus.publish().

Change Data Capture

Auto-generated events on SObject create/update/delete/undelete. Subscribers receive the diff, not the full record. Perfect when you just need "row N changed" without inventing business events.

Pub/Sub API (gRPC)

Newer transport that replaces CometD/Streaming API. Bi-directional. Higher throughput. Subscribers maintain Replay ID for guaranteed-delivery semantics.

Replay ID — the gotcha they'll probe

  • Every event has a monotonically increasing replay ID per channel.
  • Subscribers persist the last successfully processed replay ID.
  • On reconnect, they request "everything after X." Salesforce streams the gap.
  • If the gap is older than the retention window (1 or 3 days), the events are gone — subscriber must fall back to a Bulk-query catchup for the missed range.
  • This is why "best-effort delivery" on high-volume mode is not a marketing line — it's an honest statement about retention math.

Picking between them

  • CDC if downstream just needs "row changed, here's the diff." Lowest engineering cost.
  • Platform Events when you want business semantics — Loan_Funded__e, Payment_Missed__e — so subscribers don't have to interpret what an Opportunity status change means.
  • Pub/Sub API as the transport for either, especially when throughput is high or you need bi-directional flow.
Likely follow-up: A downstream analytics system goes offline for 36 hours. How does it catch up?

Sharp answer

"If the event channel is standard-volume (3-day retention) and we're inside the window, the subscriber reconnects with its last replay ID and Salesforce streams the gap. If we're outside the window or on high-volume mode, the replay ID lookup fails — at that point the subscriber falls back to an incremental Bulk query keyed on LastModifiedDate for the missed range, then resumes the event stream from now. The discipline is: persist your replay ID on every successful event, and have the catchup path written before you need it."

Deep-dive · AI on platform

Einstein, Agentforce, Prompt Builder, Prediction Builder

In 2026 you will be asked where AI fits — particularly at a fintech where servicing volume and document-heavy underwriting are obvious wins. Have one concrete take per surface.

The four surfaces

Einstein Bots

NLP-driven self-service chat across web, mobile, SMS, WhatsApp. Intents + entities + confidence-based handoff. Fintech fit: payment-status, loan-app-status, statement requests.

Agentforce

CRM-grounded AI agents (formerly Einstein Copilot). Topics + Actions + Prompts. Goes beyond Bots' decision trees — can reason over related records and call platform actions.

Prompt Builder

Reusable prompt templates grounded in record context. Output to fields, emails, or Apex actions. Fintech fit: summarize uploaded bank statements; draft adverse-action explanations.

Einstein Prediction Builder

Binary or numeric predictions on a custom object. Fintech fit: default risk, prepayment likelihood, broker conversion. But: not for the credit decision itself — FCRA/ECOA require explainable, human-reviewed adverse action.

Concrete opinions to bring (pick one per surface)

  • Bots/Agentforce: "I'd pilot Agentforce on tier-1 servicing — payment lookup, statement requests, app status. Success metric: 30%+ drop in contact rate to live agents before scaling. If the bot doesn't move that number, the bot's wrong, not the customer."
  • Prompt Builder: "Document summarization on uploaded bank statements is the highest-ROI starting point — underwriter saves 15 minutes per file. Wrap it with a confidence threshold so the underwriter still reviews edge cases."
  • Prediction Builder: "Use it for prioritization, never for the decision. Score the queue so underwriters see likely-fund-able apps first. The credit decision itself stays explainable + reviewable for FCRA compliance."

Vocabulary they'll listen for

ai__ namespace Topics / Actions Grounding Prompt templates Trust Layer Zero-retention models Confidence threshold FCRA / ECOA
Likely follow-up: Where would AI not belong in this platform?

Sharp answer

"Anywhere the output triggers an adverse action against a borrower — declines, rate increases, withdrawn offers. FCRA requires the consumer to receive a specific reason, and ECOA requires an explainable model. A black-box LLM output isn't defensible. The pattern I'd push is: AI ranks and summarizes; a human signs the decision. Use the model to make underwriters fast, not to replace them at the regulated boundary."
